Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026. This policy covers the Calendar app for Android (package uk.jj.calendar) and this website. Calendar is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Everything you put into Calendar is kept on your phone, encrypted. It is never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • Calendar does not hold Android's internet permission. It cannot go online, so it cannot send anything anywhere.
  • There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics.
  • Copies leave the app only when you make them: a backup, an exported file or a shared event.

What stays on your phone

Calendar keeps your calendars, events, reminders, stickers, templates, countdowns, shift patterns and settings in a database in the app's private storage. The database is encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256) using a random key wrapped by Android's Keystore. Photo stickers and attached files are encrypted separately with AES-256-GCM. A copy taken off the phone cannot be read. The app-lock PIN is not stored: only a salted, one-way check value, sealed by the Keystore.

Backups, exports and sharing

  • Backups hold everything, including photo stickers and attached files, encrypted with a key made from your backup password. Without the password they cannot be opened, by anyone. Automatic backups go to the one folder you picked; if it is synced to a cloud service, that service's terms apply to the copy, which stays encrypted.
  • Calendar files (.ics) you export or share are not encrypted, so anyone who gets one can read it.
  • A shared event (QR code, file or text) holds that one event only.

Copying from other calendars

When you copy calendars from your phone, Calendar reads them once and keeps its own encrypted copy. It never writes to, changes or deletes the originals. If an original is kept in an online account, such as Google or Samsung, that company can still see it until you delete it there; Calendar tells you which ones before copying.

Widgets and notifications

Widgets show event titles on your home screen unless you turn off Show details in widgets. Alerts show the title too, unless you turn on Hide details on lock screen.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. Uninstalling Calendar deletes everything the app holds. Backups and files you saved elsewhere are yours to delete.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands.
